FREMONT DIGITAL — V1 SALES AND ORGANIZATION REVIEW
October 30, 2026

The previous technical audit did not establish that the site was persuasive or
likely to sell. Its performance scores applied to an older version and are not
evidence of conversion, market leadership, or the quality of this new draft.

HIGH-PRIORITY FINDINGS AND CHANGES

1. The opening could describe almost any general tech business.
   “Better websites. Less tech trouble.” did not explain the commercial reason
   to hire Fremont Digital. Replaced with “Websites built to bring you more
   business,” followed by specific work: websites, booking, forms, and follow-ups.

2. Equal service categories obscured the owner's actual priorities.
   The homepage and main navigation now lead with websites and business
   automation. Repairs and media have their own pages and secondary navigation.
   Customers no longer browse four competing categories on one long page.

3. Geographic language sounded defensive and unnecessarily restrictive.
   Removed “All of Fremont County.” The location is now “Fremont County,
   Wyoming,” with remote projects explained explicitly. Location establishes
   where the business is based; it is not a boundary for website work.

4. Repeated process language crowded out useful customer information.
   Rewrote the service descriptions, homepage cards, process, and page-specific
   calls to action. Replaced repeated “scope,” “clear next step,” and “practical
   setup” phrasing with what customers can ask for and what the work involves.

5. Automation was a label without a concrete buying reason.
   Added a separate automation page and an illustrative inquiry-to-customer-list-
   to-follow-up flow. It is described as an example, not an installed client
   system or an unsupported claim about hours saved.

6. Real work was available but easy to miss.
   Kept the two authorized project links in a small Past work row on the website
   page. Added a secondary link to that row near the website-page introduction.
   No invented testimonial, result, client endorsement, or oversized case study.

7. Repair pricing and inquiry handoff lacked a defined structure.
   The repairs page now compares standard aftermarket and genuine/OEM screens
   where available. A quote covers the model-specific screen and installation;
   additional work is agreed separately. The selected option carries to the
   form, which asks for the device model. Supplier and labor amounts are not yet
   set, so no made-up dollar prices or instant estimates are shown.

8. Contact actions could leave phone visitors above the actual form.
   Service and homepage inquiry links now select the relevant context and move
   focus directly to the form. The regular Contact navigation still provides
   the contact information and form together.

REMAINING SALES GAPS — NOT SOLVED BY COPY OR TEST SCORES

- No verified testimonials or measured customer results are supplied. The two
  live websites demonstrate completed work; they do not prove conversion gains.
- Website packages, price guidance, and ongoing support boundaries are not
  defined. The site can explain the quote process, but cannot honestly promise
  a fixed price, turnaround, support plan, or warranty without those decisions.
- The visual identity remains restrained: white/blue, typography, and the logo.
  It is coherent, but a distinctive visual direction would need a separate
  design pass. This change preserves the approved style and fixes priorities.
- The target audience is still broad small businesses. Stronger differentiation
  needs specific, supportable reasons to choose this service beyond availability
  and a clear process.
- There is no conversion baseline or visitor feedback from this revision. The
  new headline and copy are a reasoned draft, not a validated sales result.

VERIFICATION

- Six real pages, unique search metadata and canonicals, sitemap entries, and
  native cross-page navigation. Old category hash links retain a destination.
- All six pages checked at 320, 768, and 1440 pixels with no horizontal overflow.
  Representative homepage, service, pricing, and mobile form views inspected.
- Mobile menu navigation and a legacy repair link checked in the browser.
- Local quote preview retained the model and OEM choice. Missing-model feedback
  focused the correct input. No live inquiry was sent during these checks.
- Actual-DOM tests exercise service-generated inquiry URLs, quote payloads,
  invalid category/service combinations, validation, drafts, failure handling,
  duplicate-submit protection, and successful reset with mocked delivery.
- Earlier real inbox delivery was confirmed by the owner. No new inbox delivery,
  PageSpeed score, device-farm test, or conversion claim is made for this revision.
- Published as Sites version 9. All six public routes and their canonical URLs
  were checked, along with the six-entry sitemap and legacy repair hash link.
  The live OEM option reached the contact form correctly. Empty submission
  showed required-field feedback without sending a request. The sitemap's XML
  viewer repeated the previously observed unattributed animation error; this
  was not identified as a new site-script failure.
